User's Guide

Aquaponics Tilapia Farm · Equity Outcome Ranker (Saudi Arabia) · for app version 3

1. Welcome

The Equity Outcome Ranker helps you answer one question before you commit money to an aquaponics tilapia farm: what happens to the owners' equity if things go better or worse than planned?

It combines three tools in one file:

  • Scenarios: a handful of named cases (for example, downside, base, upside) ranked from the lowest to the highest equity outcome.
  • Monte Carlo simulation: thousands of random trials that show the probability of success or loss.
  • Investor PDF: a branded report you can hand to investors or lenders.
Who this is forFarm founders, co-investors, lenders and advisors who want a disciplined, numbers-first view of equity risk. No spreadsheet skills are needed.
ImportantThe app opens with illustrative sample data. These are placeholders, not quotes or forecasts. Replace them with verified figures before you rely on any result (see Data manager).

The app runs in your web browser. It has no account, no server and no tracking. Your numbers stay on your device unless you export them.

2. Quick start (10 minutes)

  1. Open the app file (aquaponics-equity-outcomes-v3-creed-to-commerce.html) in Chrome, Edge, Safari or Firefox. Double-clicking the file is enough.
  2. Clear the sample data. In the Data manager, tap Clear all sample data and tap it again to confirm. Every field becomes blank.
  3. Enter your farm's assumptions in Section 1: capex, working capital, loan terms, output, price, costs, and your required return.
  4. Edit the scenarios in Section 2. Rename them, adjust the multipliers, and mark one as Base.
  5. Read the ranking in Section 3. Scenarios are listed from lowest (#1) to highest. Tap a metric button to re-rank by NPV, IRR, multiple, ROE or exit value.
  6. Run the Monte Carlo in Section 4 and read the probability of a positive NPV and of losing capital.
  7. Fill in the report details (project name, who it is for, who prepared it) and tap ⬇ Investor PDF. Choose Save as PDF.
Coach's tipSave your work as you go with Export data (JSON). Name each file by version, such as "Base plan, loan at 4.5%".

5. Currency toggle (SAR / USD)

  • All values are stored internally in Saudi riyals (SAR).
  • Tap USD to display every input, result, chart and report in US dollars. Tap SAR to return.
  • The exchange rate box (default 3.75 SAR per USD, the long-standing riyal peg) is editable. Changing the rate changes only the display. It does not change your underlying SAR figures.
  • While USD is selected, money fields (capex, prices, costs) show and accept dollars. Percentages, years and kilograms never convert.
Coach's tipEnter your quotes in the currency they were quoted in. If a supplier quotes in USD, switch to USD, type the figure, and the app converts it to SAR for you.

All inputs are optional numbers. Where a field is "per kg", it refers to kilograms of fish produced unless stated.

7. Scenarios (Section 2)

Each scenario scales your base assumptions with four multipliers. 1.00 means "as entered".

MultiplierApplies toExample
Price ×Tilapia selling price0.90 = a 10% lower price
Yield ×Gross output0.90 = 10% fewer kilograms
Feed ×Feed cost per kg1.20 = feed 20% more expensive
Capex ×Total build cost1.10 = a 10% construction overrun

Working with scenarios

  • Rename a scenario by typing in its name box.
  • Add one with + Add scenario (up to 8). It starts neutral at 1.00 everywhere.
  • Remove one with its ✕. At least one scenario must remain.
  • Base radio button: marks the scenario used as the centre of the Monte Carlo draws and as the headline case in the investor PDF. Choose the case you believe is most likely.
Coach's tipDesign scenarios around real stories: "Heat wave raises power cost and mortality", "Offtake contract at premium", "Build delayed and 10% over budget". Named stories are easier for investors to discuss than abstract percentages.

8. Equity outcomes (Section 3)

Scenarios appear as cards ranked from lowest (#1) to highest on the metric you select. A bar shows each result relative to zero: green for acceptable, red for unfavorable.

Metric buttons

MetricWhat it tells youGood sign
NPV of equityToday's value of all equity cash flows minus the equity invested, discounted at your cost of equity.Above zero: the farm beats your required return.
Equity IRRThe annual return on your invested equity. Shown as n/a if cash flows never change sign.Higher than your cost of equity.
Equity multipleTotal cash returned divided by equity invested.Above 1.0x means you get your money back; higher is better.
Avg ROEAverage annual net income divided by equity invested.Consistently above your cost of equity.
Exit equity valueWhat your stake is worth at the horizon.A large share of total value should not depend on this alone.

The chips under each card

  • Equity in: the cash owners put in.
  • Payback: years until cumulative equity cash flow turns positive, or "beyond horizon".
  • Min DSCR: the weakest year of debt service cover. Lenders commonly look for roughly 1.2x or more; below 1.0x means cash earnings did not cover loan payments.

Coach's read

A short written summary appears under the ranking. It names the weakest and strongest scenarios, flags thin debt cover, and gives the break-even tilapia price: the price at which base-scenario NPV is exactly zero, plus your percentage cushion above it.

Tap any scenario card to highlight it and load its year-by-year table in Section 5.

9. Monte Carlo simulation (Section 4)

Scenarios show a few chosen futures. The simulation shows thousands, so you can read a probability instead of guessing.

How it works

For each trial the app randomly varies price, yield, feed cost, capex and mortality around your base scenario, runs the full model, and records the equity result. The distribution of results is then summarized.

Settings

SettingMeaning
TrialsNumber of random runs (200 to 20,000; default 2,000). More trials give smoother results but take longer.
Random seedSame seed + same inputs = identical results, so you can reproduce a report. Change it to see how stable results are.
Price / Yield / Feed volatility (σ, %)Typical spread of each driver. About two-thirds of draws fall within ±1σ.
Capex overrun volatility (σ, %)Capex can only overrun, never come in lower than the base scenario in this model, so this spread is one-sided.
Mortality volatility (σ, pts)Spread in percentage points added to your mortality assumption.

Reading the output

  • Probability NPV > 0: the share of trials that beat your required return.
  • Probability capital loss: the share of trials with an equity multiple below 1.0x.
  • Probability DSCR < 1.0x: how often the farm fails to cover its loan payments in its weakest year.
  • Avg NPV, worst 5%: the average outcome in the unluckiest 5% of trials. This is your realistic "bad luck" figure.
  • Histogram: the spread of NPV outcomes. Red bars are below zero, green above; the dashed gold line marks zero.
  • Percentile ranking, low to high: P5 means "only 5% of trials were worse"; P50 is the median; P95 means "only 5% were better".
Stale resultsIf you change any input after a run, a warning appears. Press Run simulation again to refresh. The PDF re-runs it automatically when needed.
Coach's tip: choosing volatilityDo not guess. Base each σ on evidence: historical tilapia wholesale price ranges, feed price swings over the last few years, your operator's mortality records, and typical overruns on comparable builds. Investors will ask how you chose them.

12. Reading the results like a coach

Work through these questions in order.

  1. Look at #1 first (the lowest outcome). Could you and your co-investors live with this result? If not, the structure is too fragile. Reduce debt, add reserves, or secure contracts.
  2. Check cover. If minimum DSCR is below roughly 1.2x in a plausible downside, talk to your lender about grace periods, a longer tenor or less debt.
  3. Compare base NPV with the break-even price. A thin price cushion means small market moves decide your fate.
  4. Use the probabilities. The app's own wording treats a probability of positive NPV of about 70% or more as a favourable profile, 50% to 70% as needing de-risking, and below 50% as weak. These are planning guides, not rules; your own risk tolerance decides.
  5. Test the biggest levers. Raise feed cost, cut price, lower yield or lower the exit multiple one at a time, and see which one moves equity most. That is where to spend your negotiation effort.
  6. Do not rely on exit value alone. If NPV is positive only because of the final-year exit, the operating cash flows are not carrying the investment.

13. How the model works

For transparency, here are the calculations behind the numbers. Each year y of the horizon is computed as follows.

Funding = Capex × CapexMult + Working capital Debt = Funding × Debt share Equity = Funding − Debt Ramp = Y1 ramp, Y2 ramp, then 100% Gross kg = Mature kg × YieldMult × Ramp Sold kg = Gross kg × (1 − Mortality) Revenue = Sold kg × Price × PriceMult × (1+price growth)^(y−1) + Produce sales × Ramp × (1+price growth)^(y−1) Variable cost= Gross kg × (Feed × FeedMult + Fingerlings + Energy) × (1+inflation)^(y−1) Fixed cost = (Labor + Other) × (1+inflation)^(y−1) EBITDA = Revenue − Variable cost − Fixed cost Depreciation = Capex ÷ Asset life (years up to asset life) Interest = Opening loan balance × Rate Principal = Debt ÷ Term (years up to loan term) Tax = max(0, EBITDA − Depreciation − Interest) × Tax rate Net income = EBITDA − Depreciation − Interest − Tax Equity CF = Net income + Depreciation − Principal − Maintenance capex Final year += max(0, EBITDA) × Exit multiple − Loan balance + Working capital

Result measures

NPV = −Equity + Σ Equity CF(y) ÷ (1 + Cost of equity)^y IRR = rate at which NPV = 0 (found by search; n/a if no sign change) Multiple = Sum of positive equity cash flows ÷ Equity Avg ROE = Average annual net income ÷ Equity DSCR(y) = (EBITDA − Tax) ÷ (Interest + Principal); the minimum year is shown Break-even price = tilapia price at which base-scenario NPV = 0

Monte Carlo draws

Each trial draws normally distributed multipliers centred on the base scenario (capex overrun is one-sided), limited to sensible bounds: price 0.4 to 1.8, yield 0.4 to 1.5, feed 0.6 to 1.8, capex 0.7 to 1.6, mortality 0% to 40%. A seeded random generator makes runs repeatable.

14. Limitations

  • Simplified tax and zakat: one effective rate on positive profit, with no loss carry-forward or distinctions between Saudi and foreign ownership.
  • Equal principal repayment and interest on the opening balance; no grace periods, fees or covenants.
  • Constant working capital: it is funded at the start and recovered at the end; it does not change during the horizon.
  • Independent random draws: in the Monte Carlo, price, yield, feed, capex and mortality move independently. In reality a heat wave may hit several at once, so treat the simulation as a guide and lean on scenarios for correlated shocks.
  • Single site, single product line: no phasing of expansion, no biological disease events, no price seasonality.
  • Exit value is an assumption: a multiple of final-year EBITDA, not a valuation.

15. Troubleshooting and FAQ

Many results show "n/a".

This happens when equity is zero (for example, after clearing the data), or when cash flows never change sign so IRR cannot be calculated. Enter your capex and funding figures.

I cleared the data and the ranking looks empty or flat.

With blank inputs every scenario evaluates to zero. Enter your assumptions in Section 1, then add scenarios in Section 2.

The Monte Carlo shows a "stale" warning.

An input changed after the last run. Press Run simulation again.

Import says the file is not valid.

Only JSON files exported from this app can be imported. Re-export from the app and try again. Do not edit the file's structure by hand.

The PDF has no colored boxes or looks plain.

In the print dialog, tick Background graphics. This is usually under "More settings".

The PDF button does nothing.

Printing may be blocked in an embedded preview. Open the file directly in a normal browser window.

The Arabic text or Amiri font looks different offline.

The Amiri font loads from Google Fonts when you are online. Offline, the browser substitutes a similar serif font. Everything still works.

Where is my data stored?

Only in the open page. It is not saved automatically. Closing or refreshing the page returns to the sample data. Use Export data (JSON) to keep your work.

Can I use this for a different farm or crop?

Yes, for any similar single-site operation, provided its economics fit the model's structure (output, price, per-kg and fixed costs, one loan). Rename the project in the report details.

16. Glossary

TermPlain-language meaning
AquaponicsFarming fish and plants together; fish waste feeds the plants, and the plants help clean the water.
CapexCapital expenditure: one-off spending to build the farm.
Cost of equityThe return owners require for taking the risk; the discount rate in NPV.
DSCRDebt service coverage ratio: cash earnings divided by loan payments.
EBITDAEarnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization; a measure of operating cash earnings.
EquityThe owners' cash invested, after subtracting borrowed money.
FCRFeed conversion ratio: kg of feed needed per kg of fish grown.
IRRInternal rate of return: the yearly return that makes NPV zero.
Monte CarloRunning a model thousands of times with random inputs to see the range and probability of outcomes.
NPVNet present value: future cash flows converted to today's money, minus the amount invested.
Percentile (P5, P50, P95)The value below which that percentage of trials fall.
Ramp-upThe period when a new farm is still building up to full production.
σ (sigma)Standard deviation: the typical size of a random swing around the average.
ZakatAn obligatory annual levy in Saudi Arabia that applies to Saudi and GCC-owned businesses on a zakat base. Treatment varies by ownership.
